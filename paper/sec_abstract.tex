\begin{abstract}
Small networks are what satellites and drones can carry, and the classes they meet next arrive with a few labelled examples. We re-evaluate, under one controlled protocol, a channel score (DACIS) and a prune, meta-train, prune schedule (PMP) from an earlier preprint, against seven other ways to choose which channels of a pretrained ResNet-18 to keep. Test classes are held out: $15$ classes of NWPU-RESISC45 aerial scenes, five classes of 13-band Sentinel-2 EuroSAT-MS and, as a ground-level control, PlantVillage and PlantDoc leaf photographs; five class partitions, one set of test episodes for every method. Under PMP at $30\%$ of the parameters, the eight scores are \num{spread:es:novel_5:PMP} to \num{spread:pd:plantdoc_5:PMP} points apart on four test settings. DACIS cannot be told from random selection, and only the Taylor term is ahead of it (by up to \num{cmp:maxgain:PMP} points, on two settings), a difference that does not survive correction for the \num{cmp:total:PMP} comparisons. Episodic training did not raise the accuracy of the dense network. Pruning to $30\%$ removed \num{cost:macs-red:30}\% of the multiply-accumulates but only \num{cost:cpu-red:30}\% of CPU latency, while an unpruned MobileNetV3-Small, trained with the same recipe, is \num{d:rs:novel_5:mnv3-dacisPMP} and \num{d:es:novel_5:mnv3-dacisPMP} points more accurate than the pruned ResNet-18 on RESISC45 and EuroSAT-MS at \num{cost:mobilenet_v3_small:macs} G multiply-accumulates. For these benchmarks and this recipe, the choice of architecture and budget mattered far more than the choice of channels. A notebook reruns every number.
\end{abstract}
