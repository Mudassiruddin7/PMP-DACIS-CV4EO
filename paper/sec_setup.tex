\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data.}
\emph{NWPU-RESISC45}~\cite{cheng2017resisc} has $31{,}500$ aerial RGB scenes of $256{\times}256$ pixels, $700$ for each of $45$ scene classes from airport to wetland; we resize them to $128{\times}128$. \emph{EuroSAT-MS}~\cite{helber2019eurosat} has $27{,}000$ Sentinel-2~\cite{drusch2012sentinel2} patches of $64{\times}64$ pixels (a $640$\,m footprint at $10$\,m resolution) with $13$ spectral bands and $10$ land-use classes. Each band is standardised with the mean and standard deviation of at most $3000$ randomly chosen base-class images. To accept $13$ channels the first convolution repeats the ImageNet filters over the bands and scales them by $3/13$; a control network sees only the visible bands B4, B3 and B2 through the unmodified filters. As a ground-level control we use \emph{PlantVillage}~\cite{hughes2015plantvillage} ($54{,}305$ photographs of single leaves on a plain background, $38$ classes, resized to $128{\times}128$) and \emph{PlantDoc}~\cite{singh2020plantdoc}, internet photographs with cluttered scenes. For PlantDoc we use the cropped-leaf release ($2569$ images, $28$ label names); classes with fewer than $25$ images (one label, with two) are dropped, which leaves $27$ classes. No PlantDoc image is used for training or pruning.

\paragraph{Partitions and episodes.}
A seed fixes a random split of the classes. RESISC45 gives $25$ base, $5$ validation and $15$ novel classes, EuroSAT-MS $5$ base and $5$ novel, PlantVillage $24$ base, $6$ validation and $8$ novel. The main run used seeds $0$ to $4$ on every dataset. A second run on the same GPU type, with the same code and settings, added seeds $5$ to $9$ on RESISC45 and EuroSAT-MS for the dense and compact networks and for the ResNet-18 runs at $30\%$ of the parameters (eight scores under PMP, five under P, and the schedule controls), and ran the MobileNetV2 pruning on all ten seeds of these two sets. Cells that rest on ten partitions therefore sit beside cells that rest on five; RESISC45 and EuroSAT-MS columns use ten partitions (Tables~\ref{tab:scores} and~\ref{tab:mbv2}), PlantVillage and PlantDoc five, and Table~\ref{tab:budget} partitions $0$ to $4$ throughout. Every method of one partition sees the same split, the same S0 network and the same test episodes: $2000$ episodes of $5$ classes, $15$ queries per class and $K\in\{1,5,10\}$ supports, drawn once per dataset, seed and $K$. PlantDoc is an evaluation set only; its $27$ classes are used for $5$-way episodes after training on PlantVillage base classes, so the test crosses a domain boundary. PlantDoc and PlantVillage share some concepts (tomato early blight, for one), so this is a field-image test and not a strict novel-class test.

\paragraph{Training.}
Stage S0 attaches a new linear head over the base classes and trains for $10$ epochs (RESISC45), $8$ (EuroSAT-MS) or $6$ (PlantVillage) with SGD (Nesterov momentum $0.9$, learning rate $0.02$, weight decay $5{\cdot}10^{-4}$ on weight tensors, $3\%$ linear warm-up then cosine decay, batch $128$, label smoothing $0.1$). Stage R reuses that head for $2$ epochs at $0.01$ on the two Earth-observation sets and for one epoch on PlantVillage; at test time the head is dropped and a parameter-free cosine prototype classifier is used. Stage M keeps BatchNorm statistics frozen, trains the cosine temperature (initialised to $10$) and the other weights on augmented batches, and runs $1000$ episodes ($800$ on EuroSAT-MS), two per step, with the optimiser, the $3\%$ warm-up and cosine schedule and the weight decay of S0. Its learning rate was picked on the validation classes of the first PlantVillage partition from $\{10^{-4},2.5{\cdot}10^{-4},5{\cdot}10^{-4},10^{-3},2{\cdot}10^{-3}\}$; it chose \num{tune:best}, the smallest value of the grid, and every dataset uses that value. We then ran the same sweep on the RESISC45 validation classes and a second one with smaller rates ($10^{-5}$ to $10^{-4}$); neither was used for selection. Training images get a random crop (scale $0.6$ to $1$), flips and, on PlantVillage and RESISC45, brightness and contrast jitter; mixed precision is on. Apart from that one learning rate we tuned nothing, and nothing per method. The off-the-shelf networks (MobileNetV3-Small~\cite{howard2019mobilenetv3}, MobileNetV2~\cite{sandler2018mobilenetv2} and EfficientNet-B0~\cite{tan2019efficientnet}, torchvision weights trained on ImageNet~\cite{russakovsky2015imagenet}, feature trunk only) get the S0 recipe followed by R, without any tuning of their own.

\paragraph{Cost.}
Parameters and multiply-accumulates (MACs) are counted exactly on the pruned network ($11.18$\,M parameters for the dense ResNet-18 trunk, $11.21$\,M with thirteen bands, where the budgets are relative to that figure). Latency is measured in fp32 with one thread and batch size one for $128{\times}128$ RGB input, as the median of $60$ runs after warm-up, on \num{hw-cpu}\numif{lat:cpu}{ for the main run and on \num{lat:cpu} for a second measurement (nine interleaved rounds of $100$ passes, pinned to one core)}; GPU throughput is measured at batch $128$ on \num{hw-gpu}. Table entries are means over the runs of a row.

\paragraph{Reporting.}
Tables give the mean over partitions of the per-partition mean accuracy, with the sample standard deviation over partitions as subscript. That spread contains the effect of the partition and of training noise. It is much larger than the sampling error of $2000$ episodes (at most \num{epse:resisc45} points), so we quote it whenever a difference is small. For a pair of methods we give the mean paired difference in points with a $95\%$ $t$-interval over the per-partition differences; the interval assumes roughly normal differences, which five to ten partitions cannot test. In Table~\ref{tab:scores}, $\uparrow$ and $\downarrow$ mark a paired difference to random choice whose $95\%$ interval lies above or below zero, and a dash a score that was not run; the PlantDoc columns test the PlantVillage-trained networks on field photographs. Partitions, not episodes, are the replicates: episodes from one trained network are not independent draws of the training run.
