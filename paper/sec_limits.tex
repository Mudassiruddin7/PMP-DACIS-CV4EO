\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

Five to ten class partitions are still few. The paired intervals have a mean half-width of \num{cmp:halfw:rs:PMP} points on RESISC45 and \num{cmp:halfw:es:PMP}, \num{cmp:halfw:pv:PMP} and \num{cmp:halfw:pd:PMP} on EuroSAT-MS, PlantVillage and PlantDoc, so ``indistinguishable'' means ``not distinguishable at that width'', not ``equal''. Each partition has one training run per method, which mixes training noise with the method effect (the paired intervals include that noise), and the \num{cmp:total:PMP} comparisons share one random baseline and the same partitions. \numif{repl:mad}{Rerunning the same code and seeds on an \num{repl:gpu} changed the 5-shot accuracy of a single run by \num{repl:mad} points on average and by up to \num{repl:max} points over \num{repl:n} runs of \num{repl:seeds} partitions.} The episodic stage was tested with one recipe, and one learning rate, chosen on PlantVillage at the smallest value of its grid, was used everywhere. The compact networks were not tuned.

``Novel'' means novel with respect to the base classes, not to ImageNet, which has seen airplanes, ships and tennis courts. EuroSAT-MS has ten classes, so its novel-class numbers rest on a five-class base set, and the comparison of thirteen bands with three depends on how the first convolution was initialised. Nothing here uses coordinates, acquisition dates or radar, and nothing was run on full scenes, so what is specific to geospatial data is absent. RESISC45 scenes were resized from $256$ to $128$ pixels, which changes the ground sampling distance, and the class-disjoint split does not separate neighbouring tiles geographically. PlantVillage holds several photographs of the same leaf that we did not keep apart between support and query, so its numbers may be high.

We ran nothing on a Raspberry Pi, a Jetson or an on-board accelerator and measured no energy; only the inner channels of a ResNet-18 and a MobileNetV2 were pruned.
