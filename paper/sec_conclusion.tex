\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For a ResNet-18 asked to recognise unseen aerial and Sentinel-2 classes from a few examples, which channels were removed mattered little at $30\%$ of the parameters: eight scores, random choice among them, land \num{spread:pv:novel_5:PMP} to \num{spread:pd:plantdoc_5:PMP} points apart on four test settings. DACIS is never reliably ahead of random choice. Only the Taylor term is, by \num{d:rs:novel_5:taylor-random:PMP:abs} points on RESISC45, and its lead grows with the severity of pruning (to \num{d:rs:novel_5:taylor-random:PMP10:abs} points at $10\%$ of the ResNet-18 and \num{d:rs:novel_5:mp-taylor-random:25:abs} at $25\%$ of a MobileNetV2), where BatchNorm scale does worse than random choice. The meta stage did not raise 5-shot accuracy. Pruning saved less time (\num{cost:cpu-red:30}\%) than operations (\num{cost:macs-red:30}\%), and an unpruned MobileNetV3-Small beat every pruned network we built on the three novel-class sets.

Before pruning anything for few-shot classification of optical patches we would test a small ImageNet-initialised network such as MobileNetV3-Small. If pruning is needed, we would use the Taylor term, skip the meta stage, and report many class partitions with paired episodes. Satellite-pretrained backbones and embedded hardware remain open.
