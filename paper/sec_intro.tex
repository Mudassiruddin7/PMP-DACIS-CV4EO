\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Five labelled aerial scenes for each of five classes held out from training. A ResNet-18 trained on $25$ other NWPU-RESISC45 classes picks the right one of the five for \num{resisc45:dense_ce:novel_5}\% of the query scenes (chance is $20\%$). Keep $30\%$ of its parameters and the figure falls to \num{resisc45:A_dacis_PMP_30:novel_5}\% with the DACIS score of an earlier preprint, to \num{resisc45:A_random_PMP_30:novel_5}\% with randomly chosen channels and to \num{resisc45:A_taylor_PMP_30:novel_5}\% with a first-order Taylor score. Are such differences real, and what else moves the number?

We care about small hardware that cannot be retrained often, such as the payload of a small satellite. On-board inference already flies~\cite{giuffrida2020cloudscout,giuffrida2022phisat}, the classes that matter change with the mission, and labels for a new class are few. Pruning is one route to a smaller network, and a prototype head can then be updated from five labelled patches without touching the weights; our cost results question whether it is the best route. We measure cost only on CPUs and a GPU, so this motivation is context, not a result. The pruning studies we build on~\cite{li2017pruning,liu2017slimming,molchanov2017pruning,he2017channel} report accuracy on the classes the network was trained for. What survives when the classes at test time are not those the channels were chosen for?

We study that on a ResNet-18~\cite{he2016resnet} and on a MobileNetV2~\cite{sandler2018mobilenetv2}. Every method removes inner channels of the residual or inverted-residual blocks under one shared depth profile (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}), so two scores differ only in which channels they keep. The scores are random choice, filter $\ell_1$ norm, BatchNorm scale, a Taylor term, and DACIS, a rank sum of gradient norm, activation variance and Fisher ratio over class labels, which we also run in its three parts. DACIS and the PMP schedule of prune, meta-train, prune again come from an earlier preprint~\cite{prior2026}; this paper re-tests both with held-out classes, many class partitions and shared test episodes. The test sets are aerial scenes (NWPU-RESISC45~\cite{cheng2017resisc}) and Sentinel-2 patches with all thirteen bands (EuroSAT-MS~\cite{helber2019eurosat}). PlantVillage leaf photographs~\cite{hughes2015plantvillage}, with PlantDoc field photographs~\cite{singh2020plantdoc} as a shift check, are a ground-level control because the earlier preprint used them. They lack geospatial context, but the episodic learning rate was chosen on PlantVillage and the DACIS ablations run on it (Secs.~\ref{sec:setup} and~\ref{sec:results}).

Four findings:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item On the ResNet-18 at $30\%$ of the parameters, DACIS cannot be told from random choice. Only the Taylor term is ahead, by \num{d:rs:novel_5:taylor-random:PMP:abs} points on RESISC45, and that lead survives correction for the \num{cmp:total:PMP} PMP comparisons we made.
  \item The score matters more under severe pruning. At $10\%$ of the ResNet-18's parameters and $25\%$ of the MobileNetV2's, Taylor is ahead of random choice on RESISC45 by \num{d:rs:novel_5:taylor-random:PMP10:abs} and \num{d:rs:novel_5:mp-taylor-random:25:abs} points, and BatchNorm scale is \num{d:rs:novel_5:mp-bn_gamma-random:25:abs} points behind on the MobileNetV2. DACIS is again never reliably ahead of random choice.
  \item Episodic training does not raise 5-shot accuracy. The few schedules that gain over plain prune-and-recover are those whose score uses the meta-gradient statistic, and a variant that updates no weight gains as much.
  \item Pruning saves less time than operations (\num{cost:macs-red:30}\% fewer multiply-accumulates, \num{cost:cpu-red:30}\% lower CPU latency), and an unpruned MobileNetV3-Small beats every pruned network we built on the three novel-class sets. Pruning studies should include it as a baseline.
\end{itemize}
The protocol (class-disjoint partitions, shared test episodes, paired intervals over partitions) can be reused. Sec.~\ref{sec:limits} lists what we did not test: coordinates, dates, radar, full scenes, satellite-pretrained backbones and embedded hardware. This is a benchmarking study on Earth-observation data, not a geospatial method.
